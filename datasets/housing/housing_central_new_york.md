# Lender Center — Housing: Central New York

**Source:** U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey (ACS) 5-Year Estimates, compiled by the Lender Center for Social Justice as the “Housing Database”
**Data last updated:** March 2026
**Geography:** Central New York
**Years covered:** 2013–2023 (ACS 5-year estimates; each year is a 5-year average ending in that year, not a single-year value)

**Read this before using these figures.** Two denominators are present in this document and both are valid: **Total housing units** (Central New York's occupied plus vacant units) and **Occupied housing units** (its households). Every count outside the housing stock and median sections is a count of *occupied* units, so its denominator is Occupied housing units, not Total housing units. The source contains several errors; some figures have been withheld and every one is described in the “Source data caveats” section below. Read that section before quoting anything from this document.

Each row is one indicator; each column is one ACS 5-year vintage, newest first. Rows are split into spans of at most four years and every row states its own span, so a row read on its own is still interpretable. `—` means the source reported no value for that indicator and year.

## Central New York — Source data caveats and how to read these figures

_Source: U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey (ACS) 5-Year Estimates, compiled by the Lender Center for Social Justice as the “Housing Database”. Data last updated: March 2026._

**Central New York has no usable median in this dataset, in any year.** Central New York is not a U.S. Census Bureau geography: it is a five-county region (Cayuga, Cortland, Madison, Onondaga and Oswego) that the Lender Center defines, so every Central New York figure in this database was constructed from those five counties rather than published by the Census Bureau. That construction is sound for counts, which add up, and unsound for medians, which cannot be added or averaged. All five median indicators — median home value, median monthly ownership costs with a mortgage, median gross rent, median household income and median monthly housing costs — are therefore withheld for Central New York for all eleven years. Two signatures confirm the construction. Median home value is the only figure in this database where Central New York is not a round hundred while all four other geographies always are, and every Central New York value is divisible by 20, which is what averaging five round-hundred medians produces. Median household income and median monthly housing costs are worse: from 2016 onward the source reports the five county medians *added together*, roughly 4.7 and 4.5 times the Onondaga County figure. If asked for a Central New York median, say no median is available for the region and offer the county and city figures instead. Do not average them yourself.

**United States owner-occupied housing units for 2014 is wrong in the source and is not reproduced here.** The published value is about one twenty-first of both neighbouring years, which are 74,787,460 for 2013 and 74,881,068 for 2015. No substitute is offered; treat 2014 as unavailable for that one indicator.

**In 2013, 2014 and 2015 the component counts do not add up exactly to their totals.** In those three vintages the source's components were back-computed from percentages published to one decimal place, so a group of components can miss its own total by up to about a thousandth of it. The totals themselves are sound. From 2016 onward every component group sums to its total exactly. Do not present a 2013–2015 residual as a real category, and do not describe a 2013–2015 sum as exact.

**The indicators named “Households with income …” in the housing cost burden section are counts of households, not percentages.** The source names those columns “Monthly housing costs as % of household income …” but the cells contain household counts, so they are renamed as counts here. Never report one of them as a percent. The five income-group counts, plus households with zero or negative income and households with no cash rent, sum to Occupied housing units exactly. A cost-burden rate must be computed as the cost-burdened count divided by its own income group, and stated as such.

**The race and Hispanic-origin rows are not all additive.** Within each tenure the seven race categories — White, Black or African American, American Indian and Alaska Native, Asian, Native Hawaiian and Other Pacific Islander, Some other race, and Two or more races — are mutually exclusive and sum to that tenure's total. “Hispanic or Latino origin householder” and “White alone, not Hispanic or Latino householder” are alternative cross-cuts that overlap those seven; adding all nine double-counts households. Owner-occupied plus renter-occupied equals Occupied housing units.

**This document is a curated selection, and what it omits is absent, not zero.** It carries 55 of the source's 179 indicators. Not included: the age and educational attainment of householders, how long ago they moved in, the full household income distribution, the ten monthly-housing-cost bands, cost-burden bands below 30 percent, detailed family and nonfamily household composition, units in structure, rooms, bedrooms, and most year-built bands. If asked about one of those, say it is not in these documents rather than reaching for a substitute.

**Two further source quirks, corrected here.** The source publishes “Occupied housing units” four times, as a repeated section total with identical values; it appears once here. The source also contains a single orphan column, “GRAPI 30.0 to 34.9 percent” (gross rent as a percentage of household income, 30.0 to 34.9 percent), with no matching universe column anywhere in the spreadsheet, so no rate could be computed from it; it is not reproduced. Several column headings carry typographical errors, such as a missing space in “Renter-occupiedAsian householder”; they are corrected here and no value was changed.

**In 2013 the source does not publish two of its monthly-housing-cost bands** ($2,500 to $2,999 and $3,000 or more are blank for all five geographies), which is why the ten-band cost distribution is not reproduced at all. Use median monthly housing costs and the housing cost burden section instead.
