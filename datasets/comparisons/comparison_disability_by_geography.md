## Share of residents with a disability — where it can and cannot be computed

_Source: U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey (ACS) 5-Year Estimates, compiled by the Lender Center for Social Justice as the “Disability Database”. Data last updated: March 2026._

The disability database counts only people **with** a disability and carries no total-population denominator. A share can therefore only be produced where the demographic estimates supply a matching total. That is true for exactly two of the five geographies:

| Geography | People with a disability | Total population | Approximate share |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Onondaga County | 64,374 (2023) | 471,611 (ACS 5-Year 2019–2023) | **13.6%** (derived) |
| Central New York | 107,960 (2023) | 778,993 (derived: sum of the five counties) | **13.9%** (derived) |
| City of Syracuse | 23,911 (2023) | not in the connected data | **cannot be computed** |
| New York State | 2,394,964 (2023) | not in the connected data | **cannot be computed** |
| United States | 42,703,063 (2023) | not in the connected data | **cannot be computed** |

Both shares are **approximate**: the disability counts cover the civilian noninstitutionalized population while the demographic totals cover the whole resident population, and the two universes are close but not identical. State that when quoting them.

For the City of Syracuse, New York State and the United States there is no total-population figure anywhere in the connected data and none can be derived — the demographic estimates cover the five counties only, and the education database supplies population 18-and-over but not under-18. Report the count and say the share cannot be produced; do not substitute another geography's total.
