# Lender Center — Veteran Status: City of Syracuse

**Source:** U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey (ACS) 5-Year Estimates, table S2101 (Veteran Status)
**Data last updated:** ACS 5-Year Estimates 2019–2023
**Geography:** City of Syracuse (the source export labels this geography `Syracuse city, New York`)
**Provided by:** Lender Center for Social Justice data export `ACSST5Y2023.S2101` (retrieved 2026-07-30)
**Scope:** one geography and one period — the ACS 5-year average for 2019–2023, not a single year. This document has no year axis and no second geography, so it cannot answer trend questions or comparisons between places. No veteran-status figure exists for Onondaga County, Central New York, New York State or the United States anywhere in the connected data; if asked for one, say so.

**Read this before using these figures.** Every table below reports one indicator for one of three population groups in the same place and period: **Total** (the whole civilian population 18 years and over), **Veterans**, and **Nonveterans**. Veterans and Nonveterans are mutually exclusive and exhaustive within the Total — on every count row they sum to it exactly (5,245 + 110,180 = 115,425) — so a veteran figure and a nonveteran figure must never be added to a total figure. Each table names its own base in a bold line directly above it, and every percent in that table is a share of that base and of nothing else.

**Do not use 115,425 as a population denominator for City of Syracuse.** It is the **civilian population 18 years and over**: it excludes everyone under 18 and everyone on active duty, and it is not the city's population. A share of “Syracuse residents”, “the city's population” or “people in Syracuse” cannot be computed from anything in this document. This is the exact failure this project has already had in production: an agent divided a subgroup count by a wrong denominator and reported that 47.7% of Syracuse had a cognitive difficulty. If asked for a share of the city's population, say the denominator is unavailable and give the count with its stated base instead.

**This document does not close the open denominator gap.** There is still no total-population figure for City of Syracuse anywhere in the connected data, and none can be derived from this document. Prevalence rates for City of Syracuse remain impossible, and 115,425 is not a substitute for one.

**The disability figures here are a different universe from the Disability documents.** In this document “With any disability” counts the civilian population 18 years and over for whom disability status is determined — 100,536 in total, of whom 21,553 have any disability. The five Disability documents count the civilian noninstitutionalized population of *all ages*, which for City of Syracuse in 2023 is 23,911. Those are different statistics over different age ranges. Do not compare, subtract, average or substitute one for the other, and do not treat either as a correction of the other.

**Period of service does not cover all veterans.** The five listed periods total 4,242, which is 80.9% of the 5,245 veterans. The remaining 1,003 (19.1%) served only in periods this table does not list, chiefly peacetime service between the named conflicts. Do not present the five categories as a complete breakdown and do not rescale them to 100%.

**The median income section is dollars, not people.** Its values are median income in the past 12 months in 2023 inflation-adjusted dollars. Medians cannot be summed, and the veteran and nonveteran medians do not combine into the total median. Its `Male` and `Female` rows are dollar medians, while the `Male` and `Female` rows in the sex section are counts of people; they are labelled distinctly below and must not be mixed.

**One source label was corrected here.** The source export labels the disability status base row “Civilian population 18 years and over for whom poverty status is determined”, which is the poverty section's wording copy-pasted. That row is the disability-status universe and is relabelled accordingly. Its values are identical to the poverty base; that is a property of the source, not an error introduced here.

**The race rows are not all additive.** The first seven race categories are mutually exclusive and sum exactly to the base. The last two — “Hispanic or Latino (of any race)” and “White alone, not Hispanic or Latino” — overlap those seven and must never be added to them or to each other.

**Small counts are unreliable and no margins of error were supplied.** This export contains no margin-of-error columns, so no reliability range can be stated for any figure. Several cells are very small: 0 Native Hawaiian and Other Pacific Islander veterans on a base of 54 people, 61 World War II veterans, 41 Asian veterans, and 669 female veterans — which is why the female veterans' median income should not be read as a real difference from the male veterans'. Give small counts as the source gives them, say plainly that they are small and imprecise, and do not build rates, rankings or comparisons on them.

Each table below reports one population group against the base named above it. `n/a` marks a value the Census Bureau reports as not applicable (`(X)` in the source).

## City of Syracuse — Coverage: which places and which topics this dataset includes

_Source: U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey (ACS) 5-Year Estimates, table S2101 (Veteran Status). Data last updated: ACS 5-Year Estimates 2019–2023._

This dataset covers **City of Syracuse only**. Every figure in this document — veterans and nonveterans, period of service, sex, age, race and Hispanic origin, median income, educational attainment, **labor force participation rate**, **unemployment rate**, poverty status and disability status — is for City of Syracuse and for the ACS 5-year period 2019–2023 only.

**There is no equivalent figure for Onondaga County, Central New York, New York State or the United States anywhere in the connected data.** In particular there is **no unemployment rate and no labor force participation rate for Onondaga County**, none for Central New York, none for New York State and none for the United States. If asked for any of those, say the figure is not available for that place, and say that an unemployment rate is available for City of Syracuse only. Do not offer the City of Syracuse figure as a substitute for another geography, and do not describe it as a regional or county rate.

The City of Syracuse unemployment rate here is also **not** a current or monthly unemployment rate of the kind published by the Bureau of Labor Statistics. It is a 5-year average for 2019–2023, covering the civilian population aged 18 to 64 in the labor force. Say so whenever you quote it.
